 r=L_N^{-1},\qquad \sigma=L_N/N,\qquad

\section{Exact completed-xi and Mellin normalization}\label{sup:mellin}

Mathlib defines the completed zeta $\Lambda(s)$ and its pole-removed entire
version $\Lambda_0(s)$ with
\[
 \Lambda(s)=\Lambda_0(s)-\frac1s-\frac1{1-s}.
\]
The repository's entire xi is
\begin{equation}\label{sup:xi-mathlib}
 \xi(s)=\frac{s(s-1)}2\Lambda_0(s)+\frac12.
\end{equation}
Away from $0,1$, elementary algebra converts \eqref{sup:xi-mathlib} to the
usual $s(s-1)\Lambda(s)/2$.  Mathlib's functional equation for $\Lambda_0$
implies $\xi(1-s)=\xi(s)$, hence evenness at $1/2$.

The Lean definition
\[
 \gamma(n)=\frac{n!}{(2n)!}
 D^{2n}[w\mapsto\xi(1/2+w)](0)
\]
is therefore exactly the paper convention.  The concrete integral seam is
\begin{equation}\label{sup:typed-moment}
 D^{2n}[w\mapsto\xi(1/2+w)](0)
 =8\int_0^\infty\omega(e^{2u})e^{u/2}u^{2n}\,\dd u.
\end{equation}
Lean proves that \eqref{sup:typed-moment} implies the paper's factor-eight
formula.  It does not presently prove \eqref{sup:typed-moment} for the
concrete theta kernel; that identity is proved on paper from Riemann's
integral.

For source comparison, define
\[
 F(s)=\int_0^\infty \mathcal K(t)t^s\,\dd t
\]
with the exact kernel $\mathcal K$ fixed by the theta-mode expansion.  Direct
integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{sup:F-assembly}
 \gamma(M)=\frac{\Gamma(M+1)}{\Gamma(2M+1)}2^{-2M-2}
 \left(32\binom{2M}{2}F(2M-2)-F(2M)\right).
\end{equation}
The factors in \eqref{sup:F-assembly} pass three independent checks:
completed-zeta Taylor balls, the positive omega integral, and separate
integration of the two $F$ moments with analytic tails.
Define
\[
 M_z=2^{-2z-2}\left(32\binom{2z}{2}F(2z-2)-F(2z)\right),
 \qquad h(z)=\log M_z,
\]
on the remote sectorial branch real on the positive axis.  Thus
$\gamma(z)=\Gamma(z+1)M_z/\Gamma(2z+1)$ exactly.  The derivative tower below
is the tower of $\log M_z$, not of $\log\gamma(z)$.

\section{Rouch\'e bounds for the saddle}\label{sup:rouche}

Let $\ell=\log|N|\ge12$ and
\[
 L_0=\log N-\log\log N-\log\pi.
\]
On $|L-L_0|\le1$ set
\begin{align*}
 m(\ell)&=\ell-\log(\ell+1)-\log\pi-1,\\
 M_\theta(\ell)&=\ell+2+\log(\ell+1)+\theta/\ell+\log\pi.
\end{align*}
The elementary estimates used in the main proof are
\begin{gather}
 \Re L\ge m(\ell)>0,\qquad |L|\le M_\theta(\ell),\label{sup:disc-geometry}\\
 \left|\frac{3L}{4N}\right|
 \le\frac{3M_\theta(\ell)}{4e^\ell}<10^{-4},\label{sup:small-correction}\\
 \left|\log\left(1-\frac{3L}{4N}\right)\right|
 \le\frac{3M_\theta(\ell)}{2e^\ell}.
 \label{sup:log-correction}
\end{gather}
For $|z|\le1/2$, the segment integral
$\log(1+z)=\int_0^1z/(1+tz)\,\dd t$ gives
$|\log(1+z)|\le2|z|$.  Apply this to the difference between $\log L$ and
$\log L_0$, using $|L_0|\ge\ell/2$, to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{sup:rouche-full}
 \sup_{|L-L_0|=1}|G_N(L)-(L-L_0)|
 \le2\frac{\log(\ell+1)+\theta/\ell+\log\pi+1}{\ell}
 +\frac{3M_\theta(\ell)}{2e^\ell}.
\end{equation}
The right side decreases after $\ell=12$ and is below one there.  The
independent exact boundary audit reports $0.807$ for the deliberately wider
test angle.  Rouch\'e therefore gives one zero in the disc.

The denominator check precedes implicit differentiation.  From
$|r|,|\sigma|\le7/50$,
\begin{align}
 |1+r-3\sigma/4|&\ge151/200>9/16,\label{sup:Q-lower}\\
 |4+4r-3\sigma|&\ge151/50.\label{sup:H6-den-lower}
\end{align}
Thus $Q_N\ne0$, the root is simple, and the local analytic roots patch by
uniqueness.  A generic complex closed-disc contraction theorem has also been
formalized, but the paper retains the Rouch\'e route because the latter's
boundary estimates are already explicit.

\section{Imaginary-part and curvature bootstrap}\label{sup:imaginary}

Write $L=a+ib$ and $N=|N|e^{i\phi}$.  The argument of the saddle equation is
\begin{equation}\label{sup:arg-saddle}
 \phi=\arg L+\arg(\pi e^L+3/4).
\end{equation}
Since $a\ge m(12)>7.29$, the second term differs from $b$ by at most
$O(e^{-a})$.  The first is bounded by $|b|/a$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{sup:b-bootstrap}
 |b|\le|\phi|+|b|/a+\frac{3e^{-a}}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
For $|\phi|\le1/100$, solving \eqref{sup:b-bootstrap} gives
$|b|<0.012<1/20$.  This avoids the enormous threshold that would result from
bounding $|L_N-L_0|$ alone.

Now
\[
 K_N=\frac{N}{L_N}\left(1+\frac1{L_N}-\frac{3L_N}{4N}\right).
\]
The factor in parentheses lies in the disc of radius $49/200$ about $1$.
Combining its argument with $\arg N-\arg L_N$ gives
$|\arg K_N|<1/20$ after increasing $R$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{sup:ReK}
 \Re K_N\ge\cos(1/20)|K_N|.
\end{equation}
The stronger right-half-plane statement \eqref{sup:ReK}, not merely
$K_N\ne0$, is what controls the complex Gaussian.

\section{Contour, connector, and Gaussian estimates}\label{sup:contour}

Set
\[
 \Phi_N(u)=N\operatorname{Log}u-\frac34u-\pi e^u,
 \qquad g_N(u)=e^u e^{\Phi_N(u)},
 \qquad I_1(N):=F_1(N):=\int_0^\infty g_N(u)\,\dd u.
\]
Write $L_N=\alpha+ib$ and split this integral at $1$.  Apply
Cauchy's theorem to the finite rectangle with vertices
$1,X,X+ib,1+ib$ and then let $X\to\infty$.  The right connector vanishes,
and the exact identity is
\begin{equation}\label{sup:legal-contour}
 I_1(N)=E_N+\int_1^\infty g_N(x+ib)\,\dd x,
 \qquad
 E_N=\int_0^1g_N(x)\,\dd x+i\int_0^b g_N(1+iy)\,\dd y.
\end{equation}
The shifted ray lies entirely in $\Re u\ge1$.  Direct comparison with the
saddle main term gives
\begin{equation}\label{sup:connector-bound}
 |E_N|\le |\mathcal A(N)|e^{-c'|N|\log\log|N|}.
\end{equation}

On the actual shifted ray put $u=L_N+r$, so $r\ge1-\Re L_N$, and set
$V=|K_N|^{-2/5}$.  The central window $|r|\le V$ is contained in that ray
for sufficiently large $|N|$.  There,
\begin{equation}\label{sup:central-expansion}
 \Phi_N(L_N+r)-\Phi_N(L_N)
 =-K_Nr^2/2+\kappa_3r^3/6+R_4(N,r),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{sup:R4}
 |R_4(N,r)|\le C|K_N||r|^4.
\end{equation}
The Jacobian outside the phase contributes $e^r$.  The Gaussian majorant from
\eqref{sup:ReK} makes the quartic remainder integrable.  The omitted part of
the full real Gaussian to the left of $1-\Re L_N$ is exponentially small, so
the full real Gaussian is only a comparison integral.  Completing the square
gives
\[
 \frac{\int_{\R}r^3e^{r-K_Nr^2/2}\,\dd r}
 {\int_{\R}e^{r-K_Nr^2/2}\,\dd r}
 =\frac3{K_N^2}+\frac1{K_N^3}.
\]
Since $\kappa_3=O(|K_N|)$, the signed cubic, its square, and
\eqref{sup:R4} contribute $O(|K_N|^{-1})$ relatively.

On the actual range $r\ge1-\Re L_N$, strict horizontal concavity makes the
left part $1\le x\le\Re L_N-V$ increase toward the saddle and gives a
negative derivative of size $\gg |K_N|^{3/5}$ at $x=\Re L_N+V$ on the
right.  Splitting the two resulting tail bounds at $|r|=1$ makes their
Gaussian and exponential integrals elementary.  Together with
\eqref{sup:connector-bound},
\begin{equation}\label{sup:F1-final}
 F_1(N)=\sqrt{2\pi/K_N}\,L_N^N
 \exp(L_N/4-N/L_N+3/4)
 \left(1+O(|K_N|^{-1})\right).
\end{equation}

\section{Higher theta modes and sectorial assembly}\label{sup:modes}

For mode $m\ge2$, the phase difference from $m=1$ contains
\begin{equation}\label{sup:mode-extra}
 -\pi(m^2-1)e^{L_N+r}.
\end{equation}
Because $|\Im L_N|<1/20$, its real part on the central window is at most
$-c(m^2-1)e^{\Re L_N}$.  The saddle equation gives
$e^{\Re L_N}\asymp|N|/|L_N|$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{sup:mode-bound}
 |F_m(N)|\le |\mathcal A(N)|
 \exp\left(-c(m^2-1)|N|/\log|N|\right).
\end{equation}
Summation over $m\ge2$ is dominated by the $m=2$ term.

For the coefficient assembly, write $a(s)=\log\mathcal A(s)$.  Direct
differentiation and the saddle relation give
\[
 a'(s)=\log L_s+(L_sK_s)^{-1}-\tfrac12K_s'/K_s.
\]
On a unit step, $a''(s)=O(1/(|s|\log|s|))$, so
\begin{equation}\label{sup:two-step-ratio}
 \frac{F(N+2)}{F(N)}=L_N^2
 \left(1+O\left(\frac{\log|N|}{|N|}\right)\right).
\end{equation}
The sectorial Stirling expansion is applied to each gamma factor with a
remainder uniform on the outer fixed sector.  Combining
\eqref{sup:F-assembly}, \eqref{sup:F1-final}, and
\eqref{sup:two-step-ratio} produces the main term in the paper and a
holomorphic relative error bounded by $C\log|M|/|M|$.

\section{Cauchy transport and derivative artifacts}\label{sup:derivatives}

If $f$ and $g$ are holomorphic on a domain $U$, $g$ is nonzero, and
\[
 E=f/g-1,\qquad |E|\le\epsilon
\]
on a disc of radius $R$ about $z_0$ contained in $U$, Cauchy's formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{sup:cauchy}
 |E^{(j)}(z_0)|\le j!\epsilon/R^j.
\end{equation}
This statement, including the exact factorial and every order through six,
is Lean-checked.  The analytic input is the uniform sectorial bound, not a
finite grid.

The saddle main term is
\[
 G_0(N)=(N+1)\log L_N+\frac{L_N}{4}-\frac{N}{L_N}
 -\frac12\log Q_N.
\]
The exact saddle tower differentiates $G_0$, using $r=1/L$ and
$\sigma=L/N$.  The fifth derivative
at $\sigma=0$ reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{sup:H5}
 -\frac{6+47r+146r^2+210r^3+126r^4+42r^5+6r^6}{(1+r)^7}.
\end{equation}
At sixth order the reduced denominator is
$(4+4r-3\sigma)^{12}$.  The numerator has $82$ terms and total degree $13$.
Rather than typeset all $82$ monomials, the canonical expanded expression is
the output of the exact symbolic producer and the clean-room Mathematica
ledger; both are hash-frozen.  The coefficientwise box majorant is exactly
\[
 \frac{6422139805764931584036533551104}
 {702576099728137594188684005}=9140.8458\ldots<10^4.
\]
No binary floating-point value is used in this inequality.

\section{Four-parameter limiting system}\label{sup:branch}

The limiting equations $H^\infty+S^\infty=0$ are
\begin{align}
 \alpha^{-1}+wt^{-2}+\delta&=2,\label{sup:F0}\\
 wt^{-3}+\delta&=1,\label{sup:F1}\\
 3wt^{-4}+3\delta&=2,\label{sup:F2}\\
 4wt^{-5}+4\delta&=2.\label{sup:F3}
\end{align}
Writing $F_j$ for the left side minus the right side in
\eqref{sup:F0}--\eqref{sup:F3}, exact subtraction gives
\[
 3F_1-F_2=\frac{3w(t-1)}{t^4}-1,
 \qquad
 \frac43F_2-F_3=\frac{4w(t-1)}{t^5}-\frac23.
\]
At a zero these imply $3w(t-1)=t^4$ and $6w(t-1)=t^5$, so positivity gives
$t=2$, followed by $(\alpha,w,\delta)=(3,16/3,1/3)$.  The exact Jacobian
inverse is
\begin{equation}\label{sup:inverse}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -9&45&-24&9\\
 0&6&-6&3\\
 0&48&-112/3&16\\
 0&1&-4/3&1
 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
whose largest absolute row sum is $304/3$.

The Lean branch interface separates two certificates:

\item a center residual
$\|G_n(y_*)\|_\infty\le(3/608)R$;
\item a whole-box derivative defect

The first implies center displacement at most $R/2$ after multiplication by

\section{Finite-free orientation and localization}\label{sup:finitefree}

For ascending coefficients
$p(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^ka_kX^k$ and
$q(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^kb_kX^k$, define
\begin{equation}\label{sup:ascending-conv}
 (p\boxconv q)(X)=
 \sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^k\binom dk^{-1}a_kb_kX^k.
\end{equation}
In monic descending form, the same operation multiplies normalized elementary
symmetric coefficients.  Fixed-degree reflection satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rev}_d(p\boxconv q)
 =\operatorname{rev}_d(p)\boxconv\operatorname{rev}_d(q).
\]
For nonzero constant term, reflection maps roots to reciprocals.  Positive
interval $[a,b]$ therefore maps to $[b^{-1},a^{-1}]$.

The transported Jacobi matrix has diagonal entries centered at $V$ and
off-diagonal entries controlled by $\sqrt{Vd}$.  The exact finite inequalities
are
\[
 |J_{ii}-V|\le4\sqrt{Vd},\qquad
 |J_{i,i-1}|+|J_{i,i+1}|\le4\sqrt{Vd}.
\]
Gershgorin gives the constant $8$.  The root/eigenvalue identification is the
classical Jacobi matrix theorem \cite{Szego1975}; the interval consequence is
formalized as a typed adapter.

MSS Theorem~1.6 applied in original and reciprocal orientations produces
$[u_-v_-,u_+v_+]$.  Substituting the Jacobi intervals and the coarse
$B/D\le6$ yields
\[
 C_{\rm loc}=12+8\sqrt6<32.
\]
The transposed expression $8+12\sqrt6$ is not used.  The preliminary lower
endpoint requires a constant strictly greater than $64$; $K_{\rm pre}=256$
is the chosen exact value.

\section{Expanded recurrence checks}\label{sup:recurrence}

The terminating coefficient producer is initialized at $c_0=1$ and obeys
\[
 AC(k+1)(k+B+m)(k+D+m)c_{k+1}
 =D(k+m-d)(k+A+m)(k+C+m)c_k.
\]
At $k=d-m$ the right side vanishes, proving termination.  Acting on a
monomial with $\vartheta=y\,\dd/\dd y$ multiplies it by its degree, so the
Euler ODE is verified coefficientwise without an appeal to a symbolic ODE
black box.

The derivative shift follows from the Pochhammer identity
$(a)_{k+m}=(a)_m(a+m)_k$.  Substitution in the ODE yields the four-term
recurrence.  The expanded coefficients are:
\begin{align*}
 P_{3,m}&=\frac{AC-Dy}{AC},\\
 P_{2,m}&=B+D+2m+1
 -\frac{Dy}{AC}(A+C+3m-d+3),\\
 P_{1,m}&=(B+m)(D+m)
 -\frac{Dy}{AC}\bigl((A+m)(C+m)\\
 &\hspace{8em}+(m-d)(A+C+2m+1)\bigr),\\
 P_{0,m}&=-\frac{Dy}{AC}(m-d)(A+m)(C+m).
\end{align*}
The last line is positive for $0\le m<d$.  Exact algebra proves equality of
these expanded forms with the decomposed formulas used for estimates.

The property test evaluates $2355$ rational identities across $27$ legal
tuples.  The Mathematica clean-room test verifies all derivative orders at
degrees $5,8,11$.  These tests corroborate, but do not replace, the Lean
coefficientwise proof.

\end{enumerate}
All infinite integral truncations have analytic tails.  Conversely, no

\section{Mutation and regression policy}\label{sup:mutations}

Semantic mutations are designed to kill load-bearing changes, including:
\begin{itemize}
\item $n!\mapsto2^{2n}$, $e^{u/2}\mapsto e^u$, and factor $8\mapsto4$;
\item $r=1/L$ or $\sigma=L/N$ reversal;
\item fifth derivative substituted for sixth, or $1/720\mapsto1/120$;
\item coordinate permutation in $(\alpha,t,w,\delta)$;
\item center-only replacement of a whole-box Jacobian quantifier;
\item $C_{\rm loc}$ transposition, $K_{\rm pre}$ regression, or omission of
the reciprocal MSS orientation;
\item hypergeometric scale or recurrence sign changes;
\item deletion of $y^k$ or insertion of $1/k!$ in the radius;
\item omission of $C_{B6}$ or $N_{\rm analytic}$ from the final constant;
\item any claim of human or peer review.
\end{itemize}
Equation regression also evaluates the xi coefficient identity and the
critical-point radius at legal numerical points by an independent path.  A
string check alone is not accepted as evidence of a mathematical equation.

\section{AI assistance and review disclosure}\label{sup:disclosure}

AI systems assisted with proof exploration, symbolic scripts, Lean code,
manuscript drafting, source comparison, and adversarial review.  Review
packets were separated by analytic and algebraic scope, and later phases add
separate Lean and reproducibility audits.  Separation reduces some correlated
failure modes but does not create a human reviewer.  No report in the package
is described as human review, expert certification, or peer review.

The author executed the Mathematica cells and authorized repository changes.
The exact-computation provenance is therefore different from a fully
independent rederivation by another mathematician.  The package is structured
to make such a rederivation easier: expected values are not fed into the
clean-room notebook, directed integral tails are explicit, and every formal
boundary is named.
