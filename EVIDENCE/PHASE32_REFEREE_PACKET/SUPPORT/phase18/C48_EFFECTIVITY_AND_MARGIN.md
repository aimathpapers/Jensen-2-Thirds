# Positivity margin and effectivity disclosure

Date: 2026-08-15  
Status: fixed asymptotic margin proved; numerical threshold diagnostic only

## 1. The freed parameter moves away from the positivity wall

The exact Phase-15 branch satisfies

\[
\frac{C_n}{n}\longrightarrow2,
\qquad
\frac{D_n}{n}\longrightarrow1.
\]

Choose the eventual branch threshold so that

\[
\left|\frac{C_n}{n}-2\right|\le\frac14,
\qquad
\left|\frac{D_n}{n}-1\right|\le\frac14.
\]

Then

\[
C_n-D_n\ge\frac n2.
\tag{1}
\]

The two-thirds wedge implies `d/n -> 0` uniformly.  Increasing the same
threshold until `d<=n/4` therefore gives

\[
C_n-D_n-d\ge\frac n4>0.
\tag{2}
\]

Equation (2) is a fixed degree-dependent Jacobi positivity margin.  In
particular,

\[
\varepsilon_p=\frac{C_n-D_n}{C_n}\longrightarrow\frac12,
\]

so the new branch does not approach Holland's old `C=D` perturbative wall.
It moves a distance of order `n` into the positive cone.  This is why the
nonperturbative sign used in Phase 16 is favorable rather than dangerous.

## 2. Exact loss in the current contraction norm

In analytic coordinate order `(alpha,t,w,delta)`, the exact inverse of the
limiting Jacobian has absolute row sums

\[
87,\quad15,\quad\frac{304}{3},\quad\frac{10}{3}.
\]

Thus

\[
\|DF(y_*)^{-1}\|_\infty=\frac{304}{3}.
\tag{3}
\]

The sufficient Phase-15 condition

\[
\|DF(y_*)^{-1}(DG_n-DF)\|_\infty\le\frac14
\]

is guaranteed by the coarse raw bound

\[
\|DG_n-DF\|_\infty\le\frac{3}{1216}
\approx0.002467105.
\tag{4}
\]

An earlier AI pre-review reported the diagnostic

\[
\|DG_n-DF\|_\infty\mathcal L_n\longrightarrow10.7.
\]

If `10.7/L_n` were treated as a rigorous global bound with no lower-order
loss, (4) would first be forced near

\[
\mathcal L_n\approx4337.07,
\qquad \log_{10}n\approx1887.40.
\]

A theorem constant absorbing `n_0^2 log(n_0+2)` would then have scale roughly

\[
\log_{10}K\approx3778.43.
\]

These figures are generated by
[`effectivity_diagnostic.py`](../c48_jensen/numerics/effectivity_diagnostic.py).
The matrix calculation is exact; the input `10.7` is externally reported
numerical evidence, not a proved error constant.  A later independent-code
AI recalculation found the products `5.52,5.55,6.06,6.81` at
`n=60,240,960,3840`, still increasing over that range.  Thus `10.7` has not
been shown to be a limit or even a converged numerical coefficient.  The
displayed `10^1887` scale is an extrapolation from an unconverged input, not an
estimate or theorem threshold.

## 3. Publication wording

The theorem should be described as asymptotic with an existential absolute
constant.  The manuscript must not claim a useful explicit threshold or a
numerical value of `K`.  A suitable disclosure is:

> The proof is effective in principle after tracing the saddle and branch
> constants, but the present unweighted sup-norm contraction yields an
> astronomically large sufficient threshold.  We make no claim that the
> resulting constant is computationally useful.

This is comparable in logical status to Holland's theorem, whose constant is
also existential and enlarged repeatedly in the final finite-range step.

There is also a separate positioning issue.  The Griffin--Ono--Rolen--
Thorner--Tripp--Wagner theorem combined with the Platt--Trudgian verified
height already supplies all-shift Jensen hyperbolicity through

\[
d=9{,}001{,}052{,}027{,}541{,}590{,}755{,}840{,}000.
\]

Consequently the new content is the asymptotic shape of the unconditional
wedge, not a newly accessible finite polynomial.  The paper must say this
explicitly and must not present the current existential constants as a
computational advance.

## 4. Rescaling decision

A weighted norm or Newton--Kantorovich argument may greatly improve (3)--(4),
but a responsible optimization needs componentwise value and Jacobian error
constants.  One aggregate empirical coefficient `10.7` is insufficient: a
diagonal scaling changes both the inverse norm and the componentwise error
matrix.  The effectivity optimization is therefore separated from the
publication theorem and is not a release gate.
